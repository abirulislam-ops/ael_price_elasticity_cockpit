What this answers

How strongly volume responds to price, category by category.

What β means

β is own-price elasticity — the % change in volume for a 1% change in price. β = −2: a 10% price rise → ~20% volume drop. β > 0: price and volume move together (often supply/seasonality for staples, not demand).

How to read it

Volume is derived (revenue ÷ blended price) and prices are mixed-pack, so β is directional, not a causal demand curve. Staples (salt, oil, flour) should read inelastic; premium rice more elastic.

Elasticity by category (β = Δln volume ÷ Δln price) — Jul'24 → Oct'26

First-difference estimator over consecutive months. Teal = β<0 (normal). Red = β>0 (anomalous / supply-driven).

Price vs volume, within category — Jul'24 → Oct'26

Each point = one category-month; ln(price) and ln(volume) demeaned to the category's own mean. Colour = sign of that category's β.

Elasticity table

Method

  • Revenue = numNetValue (orders), ৳ Cr. Deficit revenue uses the GL journal (net of returns) to match the flash-report metric.
  • Category = keyword classifier on strItemName (Atta/Maida, Wheat bulk, Bran, Lentils & Pulses, Rice × 4, Edible Oil, Salt, by-products).
  • Realised net price = revenue ÷ quantity (blended across pack sizes — directional only).
  • GP% = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue, GL-level. Break-even gross margin ≈ 13.5% of revenue.
  • Elasticity β = OLS slope of Δln(volume) on Δln(price); gated at ≥8 pairs, |β| ≤ 5, R² ≥ 0.10, price dispersion ≥ 2%.
  • Price moves = |Δ| ≥ 20% MoM with ratio guard 0.5–2.
  • Fiscal year = July–June; label = start year (FY2026-27 = "2026").
  • GL map: revenue 3010001/2/3 · COGS 4810001 · manufacturing 4010001 · selling 4310001 · logistics 4410001 · marketing 4210001 · admin 4110001 · financial 4510001 · depreciation 4610001 · other income 3010004 (excluded from revenue).
  • No SKU-level COGS — per-SKU margin / a true loss-making list is not published; the price-realisation screen is used instead.
